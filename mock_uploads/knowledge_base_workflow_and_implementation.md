# Tempus Sales Copilot Demo Knowledge Base

All content in this file is fictional sample material created for testing the ingestion and retrieval pipeline. It is not intended to represent real Tempus performance claims.

## Workflow Impact

Oncology teams often prepare for short provider meetings by searching across disconnected notes, intake records, prior therapies, biomarker summaries, and external lab results. In the sample workflow model used for this demo, teams using a consolidated review workspace reduced average pre-visit preparation time from 18 minutes to 11 minutes for high-volume oncology follow-up appointments. That represents a 39 percent decrease in manual prep effort for the targeted workflow.

In the same fictional pilot scenario, nurse navigators and coordinators reported that a shared view of clinical and molecular context made it easier to identify missing information before the patient arrived. Teams estimated that this reduced avoidable last-minute chart digging in 31 percent of reviewed cases.

## Implementation

The sample implementation approach is deliberately phased. Week 1 focuses on one disease area and one physician champion. Weeks 2 through 4 expand usage to a limited set of high-priority cases while operations staff document workflow changes and escalation points. This phased implementation structure is designed to fit existing tumor board and pathology review processes rather than forcing an all-at-once rollout.

In the fictional rollout used for training this demo, teams started with thoracic and breast oncology, then expanded to GI and community sites after the initial workflow stabilized. Internal project leads measured time-to-first-use, number of cases reviewed through the workflow, and the percentage of cases where clinicians reported that information was easier to locate.

## Operations

For distributed cancer networks, operational value comes from standardizing how teams gather information across sites. In the sample operating model for this demo, regional service line leaders used a simple scorecard with three fields:

1. percentage of targeted cases reviewed through the workflow
2. average preparation time before a consult or tumor board
3. number of cases where staff reported that information was complete at first review

This operational framing is especially useful for community oncology settings where adoption decisions are often influenced by how quickly the workflow can be introduced without adding administrative burden.
